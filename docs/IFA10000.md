# IFÁ10000 — effect certification protocol

### Every effect that enters the Cotucatronics goes through here. No exception, no shortcut.
### And every board that enters an enclosure goes through track M, at the end of this document.

> **Why it exists.** Every item in this protocol was bought with bench hours.
> Freeze nearly died over the wrong number; a module spent sessions mute
> over an unchecked contract; 108 KB in the wrong place cost 13 points of CPU.
> Nothing here is bureaucracy — it's a scar turned into procedure. See
> [battle_scars.md](battle_scars.md) for the stories.
>
> **Living document.** A new effect that reveals a hole in the protocol gets a
> new step. IFÁ10000 improves with each certification.

---

## The 10 steps

Each step has an **explicit pass criterion**. An effect that doesn't pass
doesn't enter the build — it stays in the repo, documented, with the step that
rejected it.

---

### 0. CLEAN MEASUREMENT — the pre-flight, before any number

No step of this protocol produces valid data without this. Skill:
`medicao-limpa`.

- [ ] **the channel was proven**: a known echo command, the response checked
- [ ] **environment zeroed**: `tone off`, cowrie shells off, params at defaults
- [ ] **unit declared**: µs? % of what deadline? RMS, peak, or norm?
- [ ] **regime declared**: debut or steady-state? block 48 or 256?
- [ ] **one flight, one variable** — and the variable's jobs listed beforehand
- [ ] the black box armed by command at the start; a read session doesn't write

**Passes if:** the zero that appears on the screen means something.

> It cost: four MIDI test windows read as "dead link" with the log mute by a
> `bb arm` of mine — they measured my silence, and the wrong conclusion
> was **published** in battle_scars as a confirmed cause. **A zero on a
> channel not confirmed is not data; it's the absence of data.**

---

### 1. CONTRACT — what it promises, checked before embarking

Read the `description.json` (external engine) or the interface (shell) **before**
writing a line of wrapper.

- [ ] inputs / outputs: how many, and do they match the slot that will receive it?
- [ ] parameters: exact names, ranges, initial values
- [ ] external datarefs: which does the host need to fill, and of what size?
- [ ] classname / symbols that might collide with another module

**Passes if:** the wrapper and the contract tell the **same story**, and any
divergence that exists is commented in the code.

> It cost: a module mute from an `r_window` of 2048 on a 1024 FFT; a wrapper
> declaring 2 inputs on a 1-input patch; a mix param missing from an
> export. **Reading the JSON costs a minute; a mute module cost hours.**

---

### 2. EMBARK — does it wake up?

- [ ] compiles with `make check` (a clean clone has everything)
- [ ] `Init` returns true and the boot logs what it allocated
- [ ] `mods` shows the params **discovered by name**, indices ≥ 0
- [ ] zero allocation failures

**Passes if:** a clean boot, with no `-1` in any connection.

---

### 3. ENGINE — is it being driven? *(external engine only)*

- [ ] `processEventsUntil()` + `advanceTime()` every block
- [ ] warm-up in `Init` pays the debut cost at boot
- [ ] re-kick of params after the warm-up

**Passes if:** a parameter moved live changes the sound **instantly**.

> It cost the entire session of 16/08. Signature of the failure: **it runs,
> costs CPU, doesn't sound, no log.** A pure signal runs with no engine;
> everything that depends on an event stays dead.

---

### 4. MEMORY — how much, where, and with what access pattern

- [ ] how many bytes, and in which region (module pool / SRAM / SDRAM)?
- [ ] how many accesses per sample, and are they sequential or scattered?
- [ ] the size is **asked of the effect**, never hardcoded in the caller

**Passes if:** it fits with a declared margin, and the choice of region is
justified in the code.

> It cost: a reverb with 108 KB in SDRAM = +12,9 points of CPU; the same
> 108 KB in SRAM = +5,6. **The access pattern matters more than the
> size** — 48 scattered accesses per sample is the worst case for the cache.

---

### 5. LOAD — debut and steady-state, separated, in both blocks

Measure the **peak**, not the average. In both regimes: **block 48** (instrument)
and **block 256** (with the spectral module).

| | debut (µs) | steady-state (µs) | % of the deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| block 48 | | | |
| block 256 | | | |

- [ ] debut separated from steady-state (grace window)
- [ ] peak, with the meter **zeroed** between readings
- [ ] the unit is declared (µs? %? of what deadline?)

**Passes if:** the peak fits within the deadline with a margin ≥ 25%.

> It cost: freeze condemned for 233 965 µs of debut when the steady-state was
> 3 520. **Debut is the price of the change; steady-state is the rent.** And:
> the peak decides, the average lies — a module had an average of 7,8% with a
> peak of 210%.

---

### 6. TEST MATERIAL — the right signal for this effect

Each effect has its ruler. Using the wrong one produces a false "it doesn't work".

| Effect | Material | Why |
|---|---|---|
| delay, shifter | sine | clear period, audible shift |
| cross-synthesis | **broadband** (noise, piezo) | a sieve: only returns where the sources coincide |
| reverb | **transient + silence** | the tail only appears after the attack |
| gate, limiter, governor | a real percussive transient | it's the case they exist to handle |
| filter | a sweep or pink noise | shows the whole curve |

- [ ] the material used is recorded in the test
- [ ] the final acceptance is always with the **piezo**, playing the sculpture

**Passes if:** the effect responds to the right material, and the record says which it was.

> It cost: a cross-synthesis declared broken because a sine × sparse drone ≈
> silence. It wasn't a bug — it was the **wrong ruler**.

---

### 7. RANGE AND OVERFLOW — where it saturates

- [ ] a peak histogram across the range of each parameter
- [ ] measured **before** the limiter (after it everything shows 0,95)
- [ ] identified: does it saturate on a **note** (→ notch) or in a **region**
      (→ filter)?

**Passes if:** there's a table saying where it starts and how far it goes.

> It's what turns "the low end is clipping" into "from 110 to 155 Hz the
> peak exceeds 1,0" — and the fix stops being a guess.

---

### 8. SAFETY — what it can do wrong

- [ ] does it interact well with governor, limiter, and shifter?
- [ ] can it feed back through the exciter? what's the plan B if the loop fattens?
- [ ] **which params enter the cowrie shells, and which do NOT** — with the why

Whitelist rule: **character enters, frame and safety don't.**
`level`, `src_mix`, gate, governor, limiter, routing, and shift **never**.
A block that serves as a fixed reference (like the reverb) also stays out:
*if everything jumps, nothing is a surprise.*

**Passes if:** the decision for each param is written in the manifest with the reason.

---

### 9. ACCEPTANCE — the musician's ear, not the black box

- [ ] **at least 1 continuous minute** playing the sculpture
- [ ] the effect does what it promises, from start to finish
- [ ] nothing degrades over the course of the test

**Passes if:** dB says it's good. **Only that counts as delivered.**

> It cost: "auto-rand working" declared with 951 measured pushes, and the
> tap dry. **Proven plumbing is not a delivered feature.**

---

### 10. RECORD — what's left for the next one

- [ ] **TESTES.md**: a new-T test with procedure and criterion
- [ ] **STATUS.md**: state, measured cost, defects it brought
- [ ] **battle_scars.md**: if there was a scar, it gets a paragraph
- [ ] **a commit per subject**, with the *why* in the body
- [ ] docs synced in the repo **and** in the vault

**Passes if:** someone who clones the repo tomorrow reproduces everything without
asking anything.

## The executable arm — skills in `.claude/skills/`

The protocol is no longer read-only. The scars became skills that
load themselves into the session when the subject comes up:

| Skill | Fires when | Comes from |
|---|---|---|
| `ifa10000` | a new effect, revalidation, "is this certified?" | this document |
| `medicao-limpa` | before **every** measurement — it's step 0 | case 8, `tone on`, cowrie shells on |
| `instrumentacao` | log, black box, counters, probes, mscope | the 8 cases of ch. 2 |
| `bug-de-ordem` | works sometimes; an incoherent test | the 3 families of ch. 1 |
| `edicao-segura` | **every** file change | edits that failed silently |
| `aipcb` | a new board, a board in an enclosure — it's what runs **track M** | the panel board rev C with no mounting |

**Why skills and not just a document:** ch. 11 orders, in so many words,
to look for the test that cuts the system in half when the symptom repeats — and
even so four windows were spent generating hypotheses about BLE without
applying the rule. **The lesson didn't take just by being written.** A skill
loads at the moment of use; a document waits to be remembered.

## Track M — mechanical pairing board ↔ enclosure (IFÁ10000-M)

The ten steps above certify **effects**. Track M certifies **the fit of a
board in any enclosure it goes into** — holes, angles, sizes, volume, mounting
technique, access, and assembly. It lives in the `3ddesign` sub-skill,
orchestrated by `aipcb` (at stage 3, before the copper, and before the gerbers):
the full text, the anchors, and the sheet are in
**`.claude/skills/3ddesign/references/ifa10000-m.md`**. Here is the record.

**The order of priorities** — it's an order, not an absolute list:

1. **The board brings its own means of mounting.** Without holes there's no way
   to fasten it, and no enclosure solves that: here the board yields.
2. **With that guaranteed, the enclosure yields to the board in everything else.**
   It's much easier to adjust a package than a PCB.
3. **The board only yields on something else with excellent justification**,
   written, measured, and signed by dB.

**Steps:** M0 measurement · M1 interfaces · M2 mounting · M3 holes and cutouts ·
M4 sizes and outlines · M5 angles and orientation · M6 Z volume · M7 access and
assembly · M8 tolerances · M9 dry fit ⟨dB⟩ · M10 record.
