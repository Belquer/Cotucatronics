# IFÁ10000-M — certification of the board ↔ enclosure pairing

**Mechanical track of IFÁ10000** (`docs/IFA10000.md`). IFÁ10000 certifies
effects; this track certifies **the fit of a board in any enclosure it goes
into**: holes, angles, sizes, volume, fastening technique, access, assembly. It
is the core of the **`/3ddesign`** sub-skill (`SKILL.md` alongside), which
`/aipcb` **orchestrates** at the Mechanical Certification Gate — at stage 3,
before the copper, and again at the gate before the gerbers.

It was born on 18/09/2026, out of panel board rev C: **five boards fabricated
without a single fastening point** — designed to hang on the shafts of two pots
and an encoder, when the pots moved to the lid on wires and the encoder
(`EC11E15244G1`) has no thread. No gate was asking "how does this get fastened?".

---

> **"What good is an electrically correct board if we can't assemble it?"**
> — dB, 18/09/2026. A board that can't be assembled **is not ready**, with the
> same weight as a board that doesn't power on. `para_fabrica` rejects both.

## The order of priorities — and it's an order, not an absolute list

> **It is much easier to adjust packaging than a PCB.** The enclosure is a file
> regenerated in minutes and cut for tens of dollars; the board is Gerber,
> factory, shipping, assembly, and weeks — and a fabricated board cannot be
> undone.

Therefore, in this order:

1. **The board brings its own means of fastening.** Mounting holes (or another
   technique that depends only on it) are the **board's** obligation. No
   enclosure fastens a board that has no way to be fastened — here **the order
   inverts and the board yields**. A board that reaches this track with no
   fastening fails at M2, whatever the enclosure.
2. **Given those means, the enclosure yields to the board in everything else.**
   Where the enclosure's holes go, the cutouts, the windows, the posts and their
   height, the angles of the faces, the interior gap, the assembly order:
   everything is derived from the board, and the enclosure regenerates itself.
3. **The board yields on anything else only with an excellent justification,
   written on the certification sheet:** (a) what was tried on the enclosure
   side, with the measure of why it doesn't serve; (b) where the constraint the
   enclosure can't absorb comes from (the body of whoever plays it, a third-party
   part, enclosures already produced in series); (c) ⟨dB⟩ signs. If one of the
   three is missing, it's a **MUST-FIX on the enclosure**.

⚠️ **An already-fabricated board still obeys this order.** Drilling it or filing
it is "the board yields" — only after the enclosure side has been explored (edge
clamp, slot, post with a claw, cutout). Exception: priority 1 — if the board has
no way to be fastened, drilling it is restoring what was missing, not a
concession.

## What is automatic, and what is investigation

**`kicad/encaixe.py`** (reads only, never writes) runs the parts that are
measured in the file, and `para_fabrica` rejects if it says no:

| step | what `encaixe` measures | proof that it can say no |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | every connector declares what it connects (`DESTINOS`, with the sockets); **two paths to the same part** or **a socket that only powers a module already in a socket** fail as a vestige, unless declared | `--sabota m1`, `m1v` |
| M2 | ≥ 3 non-collinear fastening points (mounting holes, NPTH ≥ 2,5, and the parts in the `FIXACAO` table — only with a thread proven by the datasheet) | `--sabota m2` |
| M4 | courtyard inside the edge (`dentro_do_contorno`) | that of `dentro_do_contorno` itself |
| M6 | parts under the arcade taller than the clearance — **by the BODY**, not by the pads (`pcb_contorno.confere_arcade`) | `--sabota m6` |
| M7 | **the home of each module** (`CASA` table): surface + fastening + source. Fails the module that a connector names and that has no home, and the weight **counts how many** are without one | `--sabota casa` |
| cables | each cable between boards: 3D route along the back wall + the enclosure's service clearance, against the length in the BOM | `--sabota cabo` |

The rest is **investigation**, and it has a method. This was it, on 18/09, and in
a few hours it found what no gate had seen:

1. **The datasheet of the PURCHASED model, read in the drawing.** Text extracted
   from a PDF loses the dimensions: render the page and zoom into the area. And
   read what the dimension measures — the `10,6` of the PTV09 is the outside of
   the slots; center-to-center it's 8,8.
2. **A thread only if the datasheet draws it.** The `EC11E15244G1` has a smooth
   collar; of the PTV09, style `1` is a smooth bushing, `4` has no bushing, only
   `6` is threaded. A design that hangs on a nut the part doesn't have can't be
   assembled.
3. **Purchase ↔ BOM ↔ part in hand.** You certify what was bought, not what the
   BOM says: the pots in `COMPRA_MOUSER` were never bought, and the ones in the
   drawer were 16 mm; the MOSFETs arrived as `SI2302CDS` instead of `AO3400A`.
4. **Height of each part, with a source, against the volume — by the body.** The
   pads of `J14` passed 16,1 mm from the arcade; the body was at 11,6. And count
   the **coupled** height: the pin housing (8,85, Würth datasheet) plus the
   cable's plug.
5. **Each cable: on both sides, gender, keying, direction of pin 1, and length by
   the real route** — walls, not a straight line — plus the clearance the
   enclosure needs to open. The IDC ribbon was at 150 mm and needs 265.
6. **Fastening spots on an already-made board:** a 0,5 mm mesh against the copper
   of other nets, the bodies, and the lid cutouts, with the washer's radius. Where
   there is none, it's stated (the left end of the panel board).
7. **A checker only reads.** Never run a generator to read the report:
   `pcb_contorno` rewrote the fabricated brain board, and `para_fabrica` refilled
   the board's zones even when it refused. Both were restored from git.
8. **Writing the assembly manual is a review.** It has to say *"connects to ___"*
   for each connector — the question no correctness checker asks. It was by
   reading the manual that dB caught `J4`, the socket of a bridge that was already
   in a socket (15ez). **"Correct" isn't the point:** first "what connects here?",
   then "is it right?".

## The steps

Each step has a **pass criterion**. The one that fails stops the queue, and stays
written down with the measure.

### M0. CLEAN MEASUREMENT — where each dimension comes from

- [ ] Each dimension used has a **source**: datasheet cited, **part in hand
      measured**, or generator. The part in hand overrides the datasheet (skill
      `caixa` §0).
- [ ] Unit and tolerance declared on each number.
- [ ] The part model is the **purchased model**, not the drawn one. Anchor: the
      pot was drawn as a 9 mm PTV09A and what arrived had a 16 mm body
      (Ø16,90, M7×0,75 thread); the measured anti-rotation hole gave 7,5 mm and
      the datasheet says 7,8.

**Passes if:** there is no dimension without a source in the certification sheet.

### M1. INTERFACE INVENTORY — everything that crosses board ↔ enclosure

- [ ] Complete table: fastening · shafts that pass through (encoder, pots) ·
      edge connectors (USB-C, jacks) · windows (display) · buttons and LEDs ·
      cables and ribbons (IDC, jumpers) · loose wires · standoffs and spacers.
- [ ] Each row with the **type**: *fastens the board* · *passes through the
      enclosure* · *only asks for clearance* — and with the **owner of the
      position** (the board, by priority 2).
- [ ] **Each connector says what it connects on the other side.** One that nobody
      can say is surplus; two to the same part are the trail of a migration (the
      `J4` of the ESP32 bridge, 15ez).
- [ ] A change of type is a re-certification. Anchor: "case B" (pots on the lid,
      on wires) changed the pots from *fastens the board* to *only asks for
      clearance*, and the board lost the only supports it had without anyone
      noticing.

**Passes if:** the table is complete and no interface has changed type since the
last certification without being reassessed.

### M2. FASTENING — how does this get fastened

- [ ] **≥ 3 non-collinear points** (ideally 4), and each point where force is
      applied — an encoder you push, a connector that snaps in, a button — has
      support nearby.
- [ ] **Technique declared** per point: screw + post, captive nut, spacer, slot,
      edge clamp. **Glue only if necessary**, with the reason (the "everything
      screwed" directive, `caixa/LICOES-MONTAGEM.md`).
- [ ] A threaded shaft counts as fastening only if **the datasheet shows the
      thread and the nut**. Anchor: the `EC11E15244G1` has a smooth collar, no
      `M7`.
- [ ] Hole with the **washer clear** (radius 3,5 for M3) of copper from other
      nets and of bodies, on both faces — `confere_furos`, courtyards, not pads.
- [ ] On the enclosure, each point lands on **solid material**: away from
      cutouts, windows, and bodies that descend from the lid.

**Passes if:** the list of points exists, the technique for each is written, and
the clearances were **measured**. **Zero points = failure**, and it's the board's
(priority 1).

### M3. HOLES AND CUTOUTS — derived, never written

- [ ] Each hole and cutout of the enclosure **comes from the board** through the
      single conversion (`da_tampa()` — the sign of y lives only there).
- [ ] Position of the **shaft read from `F.Fab`**, not from the footprint's
      origin.
- [ ] Diameter = part + declared clearance; **anti-rotation and indexing**
      included (pot: ø3,2 at 7,5 mm c-c, to the left of the shaft, measured —
      LICOES #1).
- [ ] 0 cutouts written by hand next to the calculated ones.

**Passes if:** every cutout cites the footprint or the datasheet it comes from,
and the board↔enclosure parity gives 0 (`paridade_caixa`).

### M4. SIZES AND OUTLINES

- [ ] Board outline inside the interior gap, with the clearance to the walls
      measured.
- [ ] **Courtyard inside the outline at ≥ 0,5**, vertex by vertex against the
      polygon — rounded corner included (`dentro_do_contorno.py`; KiCad doesn't
      ask this). Exemptions one by one, with a number.
- [ ] Board × walls × standoffs × joint fingers.

**Passes if:** `dentro_do_contorno` 0 and `confere_caixa` passes.

### M5. ANGLES AND ORIENTATION

- [ ] Inclined face (joint 11,8°): a shaft that crosses it with the angle
      compensated or declared.
- [ ] Orientation of each connector: where pin 1 is, where the cable exits.
- [ ] Silkscreen seen through the sheet (`B.Silkscreen` mirrored) checked in the
      direction it reads when assembled.
- [ ] Footprint rotation against the cutout (the `J2` rotated 90° from rev C).

**Passes if:** no shaft, connector, or text has an orientation left to check.

### M6. VOLUME (Z) — the column of air

- [ ] What descends from the lid (arcade 40 mm, pot bodies ø16,9, display
      module) against what rises from the board, **component by component**.
- [ ] Height of the posts **derived** (e.g., how far the encoder's shaft has to
      stick out for the button), not chosen.
- [ ] Anchor: the arcade passed over the brain board with 7,5 mm clear and six
      parts didn't fit — no 2D DRC sees this.

**Passes if:** the worst case of clearance in Z is ≥ the declared margin.

### M7. ACCESS AND ASSEMBLY — and the HOME OF EACH MODULE

> ⚠️ **A module is not a component.** Amplifier, step-down, decoy PD, converter:
> none has a footprint, so none exists for the DRC, for the courtyard, for M4,
> for M6, for the manual, or for the board's BOM. The lack of a place for them
> **makes no noise anywhere** until the enclosure is closed — that's how the
> TPA3116 got to the bench with no spot (bts 15fa, 20/09).

- [ ] **Each module has a home: surface + what holds it + who says so.** Without
      the three it isn't a home, it's an intention — and *"it goes on its own
      spacers"* describes an intention wearing the face of a decision. Automatic:
      `encaixe.py` **M7** (`CASA` table).
- [ ] A module without a home stays in the table with the fields **empty**, never
      deleted and never filled in by guess: deleting hides the hole.
- [ ] **Assembly roadmap** step by step, in the order it's possible: what gets
      connected before closing (the IDC ribbon), where the tool goes in, what has
      to be accessible once assembled (USB-C for reprogramming).
- [ ] Cables with length and bend radius; routing planned (LICOES #3: the cables
      didn't go in and ended up through the grilles).
- [ ] Disassembles without breaking or unsticking anything.

**Passes if:** the roadmap exists and each step is executable in the written
order.

### M8. TOLERANCES

- [ ] Stack written by adjustment: laser kerf (`K` in the generator), the board
      manufacturer's tolerance **cited from its capabilities sheet**, measured
      part variation.
- [ ] Clearance of each hole and cutout = sum of the stack + margin.

**Passes if:** each adjustment has the calculation written.

### M9. DRY FIT — ⟨dB⟩

- [ ] One unit assembled with the real parts **before the series**: board in the
      enclosure, screws, shafts, buttons, cables, closure.
- [ ] No forcing, no filing, no improvised glue. Photos and measurements.

**Passes if:** dB assembles it and says yes. Human veto, does not automate.

### M10. RECORD

- [ ] `caixa/LICOES-MONTAGEM.md` · `docs/STATUS.md` · `docs/battle_scars.md` ·
      commit with the why · vault.
- [ ] A row in the "Certified — track M" table of `docs/IFA10000.md`.

**Passes if:** whoever clones it tomorrow assembles without asking anything.
